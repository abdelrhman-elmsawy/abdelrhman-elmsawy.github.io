Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring

Research question: Can a small set of search-performance signals rank content pages for review more effectively than a transparent hand-written rule?

Abstract

I studied whether content pages can be ranked for review using safe search-performance signals available at the decision moment. I built a Random Forest classifier using March 2026 impressions, clicks, CTR, average position, and GA4-data availability, with January–February used for training and March held out against an April future outcome. The future-decline label marks a page when next-month impressions are at least 20% lower than the current month, as a decision-support proxy rather than a causal claim. On the held-out March slice, the model reached Precision@50 of 0.66 across 331,436 evaluation rows, while a naive random split produced an inflated 1.00 diagnostic result. The final recommendation is to use the ranking as an editor review queue, not as an automatic refresh decision.

Introduction / Problem statement

The practical decision is simple: which content pages should an editor review first? The cost of a false positive is wasted editorial time; a false negative can leave a genuinely underperforming page unreviewed. The model is therefore judged by Precision@50, because the quality of the top of the queue matters most.

Data

The analysis uses the gated FlyRank internship warehouse release hosted on Hugging Face. The main fact table is fact_content_daily_performance, aggregated to content-page/month level with DuckDB. The model uses public-safe hashed identifiers only for grouping/reporting and does not use client names, domains, URLs, private queries, titles, or keywords.

The decision month is March 2026 and the future label uses April 2026. January and February provide earlier training examples. Rows without usable signal are retained for reporting but do not become positive ranked recommendations unless the scoring rule/model assigns them evidence.

Methodology

  • Model: Random Forest classifier.
  • Features: impressions, clicks, CTR, average position, GA4-data availability.
  • Label: next-month impressions < 80% of current-month impressions.
  • Validation: time-aware split: train on January–February; test on March with April outcome.
  • Metric: Precision@50.
  • Baseline: Week-4 transparent rule: March impressions ≥300 and average position between 4 and 20; rank by impressions.
  • Leakage control: features are restricted to information available at the March decision moment; the April outcome is used only for evaluation.

Limitations & honest framing

  • This is observed/measured decision-support evidence, not proof of Google's ranking algorithm.
  • The future-decline label is a practical proxy and does not prove that a page needs a refresh.
  • Historical depth differs across clients and data availability is uneven.
  • Search performance can change for reasons not represented by these features.
  • A ranked recommendation still requires human editorial review.
  • The analysis does not establish a causal effect of refreshing content.

Ranked recommendations

  1. Review high-ranked pages first: use the model score as a triage queue, then inspect the page and search context.
  2. Prioritize visible opportunities: pages with meaningful impressions and mid-range positions can be practical review candidates.
  3. Use reason codes: preserve a human-readable reason for why a page entered the queue.
  4. Monitor rather than auto-refresh: low-signal or ambiguous pages should remain in monitoring.
  5. Revalidate over time: retrain or re-audit when data definitions, coverage, or search behavior changes materially.
